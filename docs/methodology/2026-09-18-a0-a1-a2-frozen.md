# A1 and A2: frozen definitions

**Committed 2026-09-18, before any fixture inspection, tuning, or result was seen.**

This document exists because the advisor's approval of the A0/A1/A2 experiment was
conditional on exactly one thing: that A1 and A2 be *specified in advance*. The failure
mode it guards against is concrete and was already identified in this project — while
tracing where A0 stalls on the deep fixture, a "page-uniqueness / control-IDF" term was
identified that would plausibly have carried A0 past the `order-4` decision. It was
rejected precisely because it was conceived after seeing where A0 failed
(`reports/search_vs_bandit_vs_ac_dqn.json`, `hand_priority.considered_and_rejected`).
A1 and A2 are written here under the same discipline, and the git commit that adds this
file is the timestamp.

---

## 1. What the experiment asks

A0 reaches the deep fixture's final review page in **85.5%** of evaluation episodes —
more often than the contextual bandit (75.5%) or AC-DQN (73.0%) — and then completes
**0.6%** of the time from there, against AC-DQN's 84.6%. The entire measured gap between
the non-learning search and the learned arms sits in **one decision on one page**.

The mechanism is known and not in dispute. On that page every candidate is a
navigational, in-viewport link carrying an href, so every feature A0 is permitted to read
is identical across them. The only discriminator is the control's **label**, and A0's
weight on the hashed-label block is exactly zero by construction.

That makes the A-vs-B result **confounded**: it varies *learning* and *access to label
information* at the same time. A1 and A2 are the controls that separate them.

| arm | learns? | reads labels? | isolates |
|---|---|---|---|
| A0 | no | **no** | search with no label access |
| A1 | no | yes, **lexically** | label access via a fixed word list |
| A2 | no | yes, **semantically** | label access via a frozen sentence embedding |
| B  | yes | yes (hashed) | learning immediate action value |
| C  | yes | yes (hashed) | learning bootstrapped action value |

A0 → A1/A2 varies label access while holding learning fixed at zero. If A1/A2 close most
of the A0-to-B gap, the earlier A-vs-B difference was substantially about *access*, not
about *learning*.

## 2. A1 — generic lexical commit-verb prior

**Definition.** A0's score plus one additional term:

    score_A1(a) = w . phi(a) + W_COMMIT * commit_verb_match(a)

`commit_verb_match(a)` is `1.0` when the action's visible label contains any token from
the frozen vocabulary below, after lowercasing and splitting on non-alphanumerics;
`0.0` otherwise. Everything else about A0 — every weight, the tie-break, the epsilon, the
visit counters — is unchanged.

**`W_COMMIT = 1.2`.** Chosen to sit between A0's `navigational` (+0.6) and
`is_form_control` (+1.5), so a commit verb outranks an ordinary link but does not outrank
filling a form that has not been filled. Not tuned; no other value was tried.

**The vocabulary, frozen.** Generic web-UI commit/submit verbs. Chosen from ordinary web
vocabulary, not from any fixture's DOM:

```
submit, confirm, place, checkout, purchase, buy, pay, order,
continue, proceed, next, finish, complete, done,
save, apply, send, create, register, signup, subscribe
```

**Negative vocabulary, frozen.** Tokens that mark an action as abandoning or reversing a
flow. Scored `-W_COMMIT` by the same rule, because "Cancel order" contains `order` and
would otherwise be promoted by the positive list:

```
cancel, abort, discard, delete, remove, reset, clear,
back, previous, return, logout, signout, exit, close
```

A label matching both lists scores the sum (i.e. `Cancel order` → +1.2 − 1.2 = 0).

**Why this is not a hidden solution.** The vocabulary names no fixture, page, product or
element. It was not derived by reading any fixture's HTML. It will fire on controls in
every application in the benchmark suite, including ones not yet written, and it will fire
on distractors (`Save preferences`, `Send feedback`) as readily as on the control that
advances a flow.

## 3. A2 — frozen sentence-embedding semantic prior

**Definition.** A0's score plus one additional term:

    score_A2(a) = w . phi(a) + W_SEMANTIC * max_k cos(E(label(a)), E(anchor_k))

`E` is a **frozen** `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` encoder (384-dim, L2
normalised, `temperature`-free, no fine-tuning, no gradient). The term is the maximum
cosine similarity between the action's label and any *positive* anchor phrase, minus the
maximum over the *negative* anchors:

    semantic(a) = max_k cos(E(label), E(pos_k)) - max_j cos(E(label), E(neg_j))

**`W_SEMANTIC = 1.2`**, identical to `W_COMMIT`, so A1 and A2 differ only in *how* the
label is read, not in how strongly. Not tuned.

**Positive anchors, frozen.** Generic web-UI phrases describing committing a workflow:

```
"submit the form", "confirm and place the order", "proceed to the next step",
"complete the purchase", "save these changes", "create the account"
```

**Negative anchors, frozen.** Generic phrases describing abandoning or navigating away:

```
"cancel and discard changes", "go back to the previous page",
"return to the home page", "log out of the account",
"read the terms and conditions", "contact customer support"
```

**Why anchors rather than a classifier.** A classifier would need training data, and
training data drawn from these fixtures is exactly the leakage this experiment exists to
avoid. Anchor phrases are a fixed, inspectable, zero-parameter way to turn a frozen
encoder into a score.

**Encoder determinism.** The model is pinned by name, run on CPU, in `eval()` mode, with
no dropout and no sampling. Label embeddings are cached per process. The same label
always produces the same vector.

## 4. Protocol — unchanged from the A0/B/C run, deliberately

Every element below is copied from `reports/search_vs_bandit_vs_ac_dqn.json` and must not
be altered:

| element | value |
|---|---|
| fixture | `tests/fixtures/deep_flow_site` |
| seeds | 0–7 (8) |
| evaluation episodes | 25 per seed |
| episode steps | 40 |
| search budget | 4,000 env steps per seed |
| archive | `ArchiveStartWrapper`, `p_return=0.5` |
| epsilon (search phase) | 1.0 → 0.10 over 60% of budget |
| epsilon (evaluation) | 0.05 |
| evaluation start | landing page, archive disabled |
| encoders | hash (`default_encoders`) — state side unchanged |
| judge | disabled (`NullRewardModel`) |

B and C are **not retrained**. Their finalized checkpoints are re-evaluated
contemporaneously, as in the A0 run, with SHA-256 verified before and after.

## 5. The A2-vs-B decision rule, pre-specified

**This is the rule. It is fixed now and will not be replaced by a mean-based rule after
the numbers are seen.**

The unit is the **seed**, and the statistic is the **per-seed solve count out of 25**.

> **A2 is declared to "match B"** if, across the 8 paired seeds, the number of seeds on
> which A2's completion count is **within 5 of B's** (|A2_s − B_s| ≤ 5, i.e. within 20
> percentage points) is **at least 6 of 8**, *and* A2's median per-seed completion count
> is **at least 60%** of B's median.

> **A2 is declared to "close most of the A0 gap"** if A2's median per-seed completion
> count is at least **50%** of B's median, whatever the match criterion says.

> Otherwise the result is **inconclusive** at n=8, and is reported as such.

Rationale for the thresholds: ±5/25 is the coarsest resolution at which the 25-episode
instrument is meaningful (the 5-episode instrument was shown to be too coarse, and 5/25 is
one former episode-unit). 6-of-8 is the smallest majority that is not 5-of-8, which would
be a coin flip. The 60%/50% medians are stated in advance so that a large but one-sided
mean difference cannot be read as a match.

**Paired by seed** is legitimate here and only here: A1/A2/A0 share the epsilon stream
seed with B and C's training seed index, and all arms are evaluated under an identical
per-episode seed scheme. It is *not* claimed that A-seed 3 and B-seed 3 share training
variance — A-arms have none. The pairing is on the evaluation stream only, and that is
what the criterion compares.

---

## 6. Reported metrics

For every arm, per seed and aggregated: completion count/25 and rate, Review count/rate,
S1 count/rate, max depth, mean/median/SD reward, mean unique states, valid-action rate,
and errors. Aggregates report mean, median, SD, min/max and a bootstrap 95% CI resampling
**seeds**, never episodes.

---

## 7. What this experiment does not establish

- It does not isolate PER, dueling, Double DQN, n-step replay, the archive or the target
  network. Those are shared and cancel.
- A1/A2 have no training variance, so their spread understates what a spread means for
  B and C. Their intervals are narrow for a reason that is not a property of the method.
- One fixture, one budget, n=8. The deep fixture is **excluded from all transfer and
  generalization evaluation** under the current project direction; this experiment is a
  mechanistic control on a fixture whose behaviour is already characterised, and nothing
  here is evidence about transfer.
